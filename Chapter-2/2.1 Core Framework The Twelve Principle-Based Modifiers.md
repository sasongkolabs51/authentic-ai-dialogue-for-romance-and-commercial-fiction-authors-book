## 2.1 Core Framework: The Twelve Principle-Based Modifiers

Use this reference table whenever you translate a Chapter 1 diagnosis into a prompt correction. Each modifier targets a specific structural parameter within one of the five diagnostic dimensions. Select the modifiers that match your failing dimensions, write the constraint in measurable language, and stack them in your prompt.

### 2.1.1 Cadence Modifiers

| Modifier | What It Controls | Example Constraint Language |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhythm Lock** | The sentence-beat pattern (fragment-to-controlled-sentence ratio) | "Alternate between two-word fragments and twelve-word controlled sentences. Never produce two consecutive sentences of the same length." |
| **Pacing Constraint** | How quickly speech accelerates or decelerates during a conversation | "Speech accelerates by 20% when the character feels cornered. Slows to half-speed when delivering a final verdict." |
| **Interruption Protocol** | When and how the character cuts off other speakers | "Interrupts mid-sentence whenever the other speaker uses the word 'sorry.' Does not wait for the sentence to finish." |

### 2.1.2 Vocabulary Range Modifiers

| Modifier | What It Controls | Example Constraint Language |
|---|---|---|
| **Ceiling Cap** | Maximum word complexity the character will use | "Restrict all word choices to monosyllabic or two-syllable words. No abstract nouns. No softening qualifiers." |
| **Floor Mandate** | Minimum register the character will drop to | "Maintain technical precision. No slang. No contractions. Every term must be the exact specification." |
| **Domain Lock** | The semantic field the character draws from | "Vocabulary restricted to domestic, tactile language. No corporate jargon. No academic phrasing." |

### 2.1.3 Sentence-Length Variance Modifiers

| Modifier | What It Controls | Example Constraint Language |
|---|---|---|
| **Variance Band** | Absolute minimum and maximum sentence length | "No sentence shorter than two words. No sentence longer than twenty words. All sentences must fall within this band." |
| **Distribution Rule** | How different lengths alternate within a speech block | "Alternate between 2–4 word bursts and one longer controlled sentence (15–20 words). No two consecutive sentences may share the same length." |

### 2.1.4 Emotional Register Modifiers

| Modifier | What It Controls | Example Constraint Language |
|---|---|---|
| **Baseline Temperature** | Default emotional output level | "Cold and controlled. Irritation surfaces only through clipped syntax and physical action beats. No warm or collaborative language." |
| **Leakage Rate** | How much true feeling bleeds through the defensive surface per exchange | "One microexpression of vulnerability per exchange, immediately suppressed. The character says 'it's fine' while the reader feels it is not." |

### 2.1.5 Conversational Rhythm Modifiers

| Modifier | What It Controls | Example Constraint Language |
|---|---|---|
| **Turn-Taking Style** | Whether the character waits, interrupts, deflects, or monologues | "Interrupts. Deflects questions with counter-questions. Shuts down the exchange before the other character finishes." |
| **Exit Pattern** | How the character ends their contributions to an exchange | "Trails off midsentence. Walks away before the other speaker can respond. Never says goodbye." |
