## 2.3 Case Application: Before-and-After Transformation

Use this worked example as a calibration reference when stacking your own modifiers. It demonstrates how five targeted constraints transform a flat output into character-specific dialogue in a single generation pass.

**Scene:** Grumpy hero Callum responds to Mara spilling coffee on his manuscript.

**Modifiers Applied:**
- Rhythm Lock → "Fragments of two to four words, then one controlled sentence of twelve to fifteen words"
- Ceiling Cap → "Monosyllabic and blunt"
- Baseline Temperature → "Cold, controlled irritation"
- Leakage Rate → "One microexpression of vulnerability per exchange, immediately suppressed"
- Turn-Taking Style → "Interrupts, does not wait for the other speaker to finish"

**Before (flat output):**
> *"Oh, that's quite all right. I appreciate you being honest about it. Perhaps we can find a way to clean this up together. I understand these things happen."*

**After (corrected output):**
> *"Stop. Just—stop. Don't apologize. I don't need your 'I'm so sorry, let me help you clean this up.' I need you to move your hands away from the pages. Now. [He's already lifting the manuscript, checking the water damage, jaw tight.] It's fine. It's fine. Go."*

**Dimension-by-Dimension Shift:**

| Dimension | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Cadence | Uniform, unhurried, four identical beats | Jagged: fragments snap against one longer controlled line |
| Vocabulary | Polite, mid-register, collaborative | Blunt, monosyllabic, stripped of courtesy |
| Sentence-Length Variance | All 12–15 words; metronomic | Two words → fifteen → three; dynamic and breathing |
| Emotional Register | Warm, forgiving | Cold, guarded; one micro-leak in the repeated "It's fine" |
| Conversational Rhythm | Waits; responds fully; invites collaboration | Interrupts; shuts down; dismisses before apology completes |
