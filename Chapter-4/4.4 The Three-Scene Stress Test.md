## 4.4 The Three-Scene Stress Test

### 4.4.1 Action Plan

1. Open your AI tool. Paste your completed Dialogue Matrix into the system prompt or character instruction field.
2. Generate Scene One (Conflict). Read the output. Set it aside.
3. Generate Scene Two (Flirtation). Read the output. Set it aside.
4. Generate Scene Three (Vulnerability). Read the output. Set it aside.
5. Run the Ninety-Second Voice Verification Read (from Chapter 2) on each scene. Log your results.
6. Identify any drifting fields. Adjust one field at a time. Regenerate. Reverify.
7. Repeat until all three scenes hold. Confirm by reading all three back-to-back.

### 4.4.4 Decision Tree: Diagnosing a Drifting Scene

**Step 1 – Which scene drifted?**

- Conflict → Proceed to Step 2a.
- Flirtation → Proceed to Step 2b.
- Vulnerability → Proceed to Step 2c.

**Step 2a – Conflict scene drift:**

| Symptom | Failing Field | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Generic anger, shouting, name-calling | Emotional Default | Add a wound-specific trigger-response clause |
| Too calm, too measured, no heat | Sentence Architecture | Add: "Under conflict: reduce sentence length by 50%. Increase fragments." |
| Vocabulary shifts to formal or academic | Vocabulary Ceiling | Add: "Under conflict: maintain default register. Do not shift to formal distancing." |

**Step 2b – Flirtation scene drift:**

| Symptom | Failing Field | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Too smooth, too charming, no tension | Conversational Rhythm | Add: "Under attraction: turn-taking loosens. Sentences trail. Pauses lengthen." |
| Identical to baseline, no shift at all | Emotional Default | Increase the leakage rate for attraction contexts |
| Overly verbal, too many words | Cadence Signature | Add: "Under attraction: sentences lengthen by 20%. Pauses become loaded." |

**Step 2c – Vulnerability scene drift:**

| Symptom | Failing Field | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sentimental, overwrought, melodramatic | Cadence Signature | Add: "When defenses crack: revert to two- to four-word fragments. Trail off. Leave thoughts incomplete." |
| Identical to baseline, no crack visible | Signature Tics or Emotional Default | Reference the Layer Seven inversion explicitly |
| Too articulate, too composed | Vocabulary Ceiling | Add: "Under vulnerability: drop to simplest available words. No polysyllabic distancing." |

### 4.4.5 Overconstraining Self-Assessment

Read your three test scenes back-to-back and answer each question. Your matrix should produce a recognizable voice that modulates with emotion, like a single instrument playing different dynamics.

- [ ] Can I hear the same character in all three scenes? (The timbre holds.)
- [ ] Does the voice shift noticeably between scenes? (The dynamics change.)
- [ ] Is the conflict scene measurably different in rhythm or vocabulary from the flirtation scene? (The instrument responds to the music.)
- [ ] Does the vulnerability scene feel like the same person with their armor removed, rather than a different person entirely? (The mask cracks; the human underneath is recognizable.)
- [ ] If I removed the scene labels, could I still tell which scene was which based on the speech patterns alone? (The modulation is audible.)

When all five boxes are checked, your matrix is balanced. If any box fails, find the field that feels the most rigid, loosen one constraint in that field, then regenerate and reread.
