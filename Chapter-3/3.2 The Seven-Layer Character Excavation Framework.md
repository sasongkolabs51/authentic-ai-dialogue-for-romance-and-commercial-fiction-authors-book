### 3.2.5 Layer Five: Core Wound Excavation

**Purpose:** Name the deepest psychological injury governing the character's emotional baseline. This is the single most important layer for subtext.

**Character:** [CHARACTER_NAME]

**1. The Wound Belief:** In one sentence, what does this character believe about themselves that was installed by their deepest injury?

> [YOUR_ANSWER — E.G., "My needs are an inconvenience to the people I love."]

**2. The Conversational Cost:** How does holding this belief make every act of speaking more difficult, more effortful, or more guarded?

> [YOUR_ANSWER — E.G., "Every sentence that expresses a want costs enormous effort. He minimizes his own desires to reduce the burden he believes he imposes."]

**3. The Linguistic Fingerprint:** What specific, repeatable speech pattern does this wound produce? Name the vocabulary shift, sentence-length change, or tonal quality.

> [YOUR_ANSWER — E.G., "Requests emerge as clipped, reluctant fragments. 'Stay, then' rather than 'I need you to stay.' Apologetic brevity. A shrug baked into the syntax."]

**4. The Subtext Test:** Write one line of dialogue where the wound is visible beneath the words. Then write the same line as the character wishes they could say it, without the wound filtering it.

- **Wound-filtered:** [E.G., "Stay, then."]
- **Unfiltered wish:** [E.G., "Please don't go. I need you here. I can't do this alone."]

The gap between these two lines is your subtext engine. Every scene where the reader senses what the character cannot say draws its power from this distance.
