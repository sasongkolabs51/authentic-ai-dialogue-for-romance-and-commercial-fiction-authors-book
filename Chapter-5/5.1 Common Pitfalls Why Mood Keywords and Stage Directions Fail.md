## 5.1 Common Pitfalls: Why Mood Keywords and Stage Directions Fail

Before building the filter, confirm you are not defaulting to one of these three structurally broken approaches. Each one fails for a specific reason that the Three-Tier Emotional Filter is designed to solve.

| Approach | What You Did | Why It Fails Structurally | What the AI Actually Received |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bracketed stage directions** | Typed [Callum is hiding fear beneath irritation] into the prompt | The AI treats brackets as metadata or converts them to narration; it does not build the feeling into rhythm, word choice, or pauses | A description of a feeling; no instructions for how the feeling reshapes speech |
| **Revision-pass emotion injection** | Generated dialogue first, then rewrote lines to add emotional weight | Emotion in dialogue is structural; it lives in the architecture of the sentence at generation time | Flat sentences with painted-on sentiment added afterward |
| **Mood keywords** | Typed "angry," "flirty," or "vulnerable" into the prompt | Keywords specify an emotional color but not how that color deepens, shifts, cracks, or bleeds through as the scene progresses | A one-note performance with no progression, no mask slip, no dynamic shift |

**The Core Principle:** Stop describing feelings. Start constraining how feelings reshape speech. The Three-Tier Emotional Filter encodes emotion as structural instructions the AI executes during generation, producing subtext, tension, and progression in the output itself.
