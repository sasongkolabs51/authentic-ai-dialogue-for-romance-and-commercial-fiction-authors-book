## 6.1 Self-Assessment: Identifying Your Subgenre

Answer the three questions below and note which subgenre column you check most often. That is your starting calibration.

**1. When emotional tension peaks in your manuscript, does the scene:**

- [ ] Resolve within the same chapter, with warmth and controlled spikes → **Contemporary Romance** lean
- [ ] Sustain for multiple chapters, building to a sharp, potentially devastating crack → **Dark Romance** lean
- [ ] Cycle quickly through deflection, humor, and brief moments of sincerity → **Rom-Com** lean
- [ ] Get interrupted by external danger, creating an uneven push-pull rhythm → **Romantic Suspense** lean
- [ ] Stretch across multiple scenes, delayed by social convention and propriety → **Historical Romance** lean

**2. When your characters speak, the unspoken meaning underneath their words is:**

- [ ] Visible to the reader within two to three exchanges → **Contemporary Romance** lean
- [ ] Partially hidden for five to eight exchanges, sensed rather than seen → **Dark Romance** lean
- [ ] Playful, light, and woven through the humor → **Rom-Com** lean
- [ ] Layered with trust and suspicion in every exchange → **Romantic Suspense** lean
- [ ] Carrying most of the emotional weight because direct expression is forbidden → **Historical Romance** lean

**3. The emotional release in your scenes arrives:**

- [ ] Steadily, within the scene itself → **Contemporary Romance** lean
- [ ] After a long build, then suddenly and sharply → **Dark Romance** lean
- [ ] In brief, sharp moments between rapid exchanges → **Rom-Com** lean
- [ ] Unevenly, interrupted by plot pressure → **Romantic Suspense** lean
- [ ] Slowly, delayed by what cannot be said aloud → **Historical Romance** lean
